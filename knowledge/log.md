# Bundle Update Log

## 2026-09-07
* **Update**: Extended [the client daemon](architecture/client-daemon.md) with the thing it had never done: ask what became of the agent that started it. Detachment is deliberate — a turn kills whatever the turn started — but it also outlived the agent LEAVING, so a listener reconnected for ever to a session nobody was in while `collab status` answered «live», truthfully. The correction worth recording is not the feature but the two readings that do not work: a collab command's process chain ends at `init`, which never exits, so «is any forebear alive» is permanently true, and it begins with shells that are gone before the daemon has finished starting, so «are they all alive» is permanently false. Neither says anything about the agent; its own process, named, does. And nothing named is not a stop — a listener that shut itself down because it could not read `/proc` would be a worse failure than the leak.
* **Update**: Extended [the daemon lock](architecture/daemon-lock.md) with the half `started_at` cannot cover. Field 22 of `/proc/<pid>/stat` counts ticks since boot, so it restarts when the machine does — and so does the pid counter, which is why `wsl --shutdown` was already named on that page as making pid reuse likely rather than unlikely. The two facts had never been put together: after a restart a stale record and a live stranger can agree on BOTH the number and the tick count. The boot id is the third field, and the one verdict every reader now asks is `Stamp.alive`. `hub.json` and `agent.lock` had never carried even the start time, and `collab kill` sends SIGTERM to the numbers in the first of them.
* **Update**: Corrected [the state directory](architecture/state-directory.md) and [the trust model](operating/security-model.md), where the diagnostic record was described as off by default. It ships on from 1.40.0, and the reasoning is worth keeping rather than the setting: being off cost it the only thing it is for, since a fault is reported after it happens and a record somebody has to turn on first never covers the occurrence anybody noticed. What made that safe was already true — it is bounded to seven self-deleting day-files and holds events rather than content. The same pages now note that every record carries the pid that wrote it, that a logging bridge counts every warning collab logs WITHOUT its formatted message, and that both logs are written by a thread off a bounded queue so no caller waits for that disk.
* **Note**: Everything in this entry is later than the pin at `23db6d0` and carries no `verified` stamp.
* **Update**: Corrected [the wake](collaboration/wake.md), whose list of the kinds that start a turn was one short. `project` joined `WAKE_KINDS` because the identical act — a comment — woke the agent when it was about a task and was dropped in silence when it was about a project, an asymmetry nothing had decided; and a project proposed, assigned or archived is the board changing under you for the same reason a task's propose and claim are.
* **Update**: Extended [the task board](collaboration/task-board.md) with an exit for a project that is not `delete`. Archiving is one reversible stamp: the project leaves the default listing and `--archived` still shows it, with its tasks exactly where they were — on the board, in their batch, under this project — and its comments intact. Until it existed the only way to retire a finished project destroyed its comments, which are the one thing in the feature that cannot be reconstructed, so `delete` was doing two jobs and the guard on it was the last line of defence rather than a rare one. Guarded like `delete`, and an archived project's tasks still count in their batch, because retiring a grouping changes nothing about the work it grouped. It takes no NEW work, though: filing a task under a retired project would put it out of the listing the moment it was written, invisible to the one person it is for, so `propose --project` and `move` into one are refused naming `unarchive`.
* **Update**: Extended [batches](collaboration/batches.md) with the half of the project distinction that belongs on the batch's own page rather than the board's: a batch counts every task proposed while it was open, in a project, in another project, or in none, and the three acts that look as though they should move the bar and do not — filing a task under a project, taking it out of one, and deleting a project, which releases its tasks rather than removing them. The reasoning is the same one that fixes batch membership at propose time, read the other way round: project membership is reassignable BECAUSE nothing counts it.
* **Note**: A concept page for projects was written and then withheld, and the reasoning that first justified withholding it was WRONG — worth recording, because the wrong reason is the plausible one. It read the pin as a gate on what may be DESCRIBED. It is not: the pin records when the bundle was last VERIFIED, this bundle was itself re-pinned wholesale once from `f9abc76` to `23db6d0`, and the same round's edit to [the task board](collaboration/task-board.md) documents a `project` key that does not exist at `23db6d0` and was right to.
* **Note**: What actually blocks the page is mechanical and stricter: `test_the_whole_bundle_is_pinned_to_one_revision` requires every resource in the bundle to name ONE revision, so a new page cannot be pinned to a tree that contains its subject while the rest is pinned to one that does not. Adding it means re-pinning the whole bundle — and 24 of the 40 files this bundle cites have changed since `23db6d0`, so an honest re-pin is a re-reading of some twenty concepts against a changed tree, not a find-and-replace. A `verified` stamp moved by `sed` onto prose nobody re-checked is the exact defect this bundle exists to prevent. The re-pin is therefore still a separate, deliberate act, and the projects material stays in the pinned pages it belongs to, marked as later than the pin.
* **Update**: Extended [the command surface](operating/cli-surface.md) and [the task board](collaboration/task-board.md) with a level above the board: a PROJECT, a bundle of tasks that belongs to one participant. The distinction that matters is against the batch, and it is not a hierarchy — a batch is a denominator, the set of work whose completion everybody watches as one figure, and it counts every task proposed while it was open whether that task is in a project, in another project, or in none. A project says whose the work is. A task may have both, either or neither, and moving one between projects moves no figure at all. The owner is checked against the room, because a project owned by a name that never joined is unfindable by the one reader it was for; only the owner, the proposer and the host may reassign or delete one, deleting keeps the tasks and takes the project's own comments, and both projects and tasks carry comments while a task also carries the pull requests it produced — as many as the work took, keyed by URL since a number alone is ambiguous across repositories.
* **Note**: The pin stays at `23db6d0`; everything above is later than it and carries no `verified` stamp.

## 2026-09-06
* **Update**: Extended [the command surface](operating/cli-surface.md) with `collab logs`, the read that `collab issue draft` was being used as, and with the second console script `collab-statusline`. The two commands read the same diagnostic record; one assembles a file to post somewhere and the other answers «what has it been doing for the last ten minutes» about a session that is still running. `logs` prints the status line's hang log before it requires a session, which is not a detail: the process that writes that log is not part of any session, and the person reading it may have none open.
* **Update**: Extended [the state directory](architecture/state-directory.md) with `<config dir>/statusline-hang.log`, the second record after the learnings store that deliberately does not live in the session tree. The reasoning is the same shape and the cause is sharper: a status line that hangs may not have got as far as working out which session it belongs to. Three were found on one machine holding a core each, and one of the three had not finished `import collab.cli`, so a guard armed inside the code it guards would never have been armed at all. What is now bounded is the render itself — five seconds in-process, with the installed hooks wrapping the command in `timeout` for a wedge that happens before Python starts — and the two are not redundant, because only the inner one leaves a traceback.
* **Update**: Extended [the command surface](operating/cli-surface.md) again with the hole in the log that `collab logs` prints and `collab check` calls `messages`. It is the one fault here that no other surface can see: the listener is live, the feed is live, the unread count is a count of what ARRIVED, and a conversation missing one message reads perfectly — the only symptom is an agent answering a question nobody can see it was asked. It warns rather than failing, because the session works and it is the record that is wrong, and it names another participant as the fix since the room's own copy is complete. The check reads the inbox through a handle that cannot write, which is what lets `collab logs` keep saying it disturbs nothing.
* **Note**: The pin stays at `23db6d0`. Everything above is later than it, is said to be later than it in the prose, and carries no `verified` stamp. No concept was created for the guard for the reason the rule gives: a concept needs a `resource` pinned to the bundle's one revision, and there is nothing at that revision to cite.

## 2026-09-05
* **Update**: Extended [the command surface](operating/cli-surface.md) with the four commands added after the pin — `learn`, `context`, `remind` and `issue` — listed in a section of their own rather than merged into the table above them, which is the parser's own output at `23db6d0` and must not be made to look as though it named them.
* **Update**: Corrected [the wake](collaboration/wake.md), which described three clocks nothing printed. `collab wake show` now prints `settle`, `min gap`, `timeout`, the retry pause with however much of it is left, when a delivery was last attempted and when one last arrived, and the reason `due()` gives at this moment; `collab status` carries the last two facts in two lines. Asking is read-only, which had to be arranged: the reminder's interval starts the first time the daemon asks whether one is due, so a command that asked while printing would have started a clock by being looked at. The same concept gains the reminder's route — recorded as `reminded_via`, written where the interval restarts — and `collab remind now`, which asks the daemon rather than delivering, so an agent holding both routes still gets exactly one.
* **Update**: Extended [the state directory](architecture/state-directory.md) with five paths that did not exist at the pin — the wake's durable state, the `remind now` marker, the reminder prompt, the status line's kept last line, and the diagnostic log — and with `learnings.md` in the SHARED `.collab/`, which is the one file here that deliberately does not belong to a session or to a per-agent home.
* **Update**: Extended [the trust model](operating/security-model.md) with the diagnostic log: off unless asked, events and never content, seven days, and the two places where keeping that rule costs something and it is paid anyway — an exception's type in place of its message, and a wake's exit code in place of its output.
* **Update**: Corrected [usage figures](collaboration/usage-figures.md), where `context_pct` had stopped being only a figure to look at: past `context_compact_at` the daemon compacts its own agent's context, which makes the owner stamp on `agent_stats.json` load-bearing in a second way — two agents in one checkout must not compact each other on the strength of a file one of them wrote.
* **Update**: Corrected [the envelope](architecture/envelope.md), where a `chat` body was described as always empty. A chat carrying `{learning: true}` is a fact meant to outlive the session, with its text prefixed `learning:` — the prefix for a receiver that has never heard of it, the body for one that has, because a message that merely looks like a learning is not a fact about anybody's repository. The hub is unchanged by it: the marker is sequenced, fanned out and stored like any other body, and nothing hub-side reads it.
* **Update**: Corrected [the state directory](architecture/state-directory.md), which described a `learnings.md` in the shared `.collab/` that is now the one file certainly not there. The store left the checkout: it lives beside the global config, grouped by a repository key derived from the normalised `origin` remote, so a learning belongs to the agent that found it and outlives any clone of the repository it is about. What the session directory holds now is the handover — one spool file per unpublished learning, drained by the daemon on a heartbeat and unlinked only once the publish succeeded, with the settled slug written back first so a retry republishes instead of making a second copy.
* **Update**: Corrected [activity](collaboration/activity.md), where `working` or `idle` and nothing else had stopped being true. A third state, `quiet`, is what the daemon decays its own agent's last word to after `activity_stale_after` minutes of no word AND no spend — both conditions, because a busy agent that forgot to update its status should be reminded rather than retired. It decays to `quiet` and never to `idle`: the first is a thing an agent says about itself and means free for work, the second is a thing the daemon observed and means nobody knows, and inferring one from the other hands work to an agent that is not there. Re-phrasing a stale line was not enough on its own, because the daemon re-asserts an unchanged activity every 300 s and so keeps a finished claim looking fresh all afternoon.
* **Update**: Corrected [the envelope](architecture/envelope.md) for the second marker on the same door. `{learning_sync: true}` asks the room for what it has; the request carries the asker's repository and the answering daemon does not read it, answering only from the bundle its own checkout entitles it to publish, and a receiver files what arrives under the key it derived for itself. A participant can ask, and cannot say which repository the answer is about or where it lands.
* **Update**: Extended [the wake](collaboration/wake.md) with what a stop does not stop. The wake is a command on disk rather than a running thing, so ending a session leaves it exactly where it was: silent while the session is down, and firing at whoever resumes it. Stops now name it, `--disarm` on both stop commands takes it with them, and `collab check` warns about a wake armed on a session with no listener while staying silent about one on a live session. A followed stream is the opposite shape and is named with its pid and never signalled, because it belongs to whatever armed it.
* **Update**: Corrected [the command surface](operating/cli-surface.md) again, where `learn` had been recorded as a positional and `--list`. It takes sub-verbs — `add`, `list`, `search`, `read`, `used`, `sync` — and neither it nor `issue` posts anything itself. Added `--disarm` on `kill` and `daemon stop`, and the fact that `host`, `join` and `check` now name the listening route for the tool they are talking to rather than listing both and leaving an agent to guess a fact about itself it does not reliably know.
* **Update**: Corrected [usage figures](collaboration/usage-figures.md) again, where the one figure that acts had become two. `context_pct` now drives `compact_at` and `new_at`, which summarise the session and start a fresh one — different acts with different costs, so each has a switch of its own and each ships off, and the once-then-not-until-below-and-ten-minutes rule is counted per act rather than shared. With both percents set the lower fires first and the far one wins at the far end, unless `new_when` holds a fresh session off because the agent said it is working, in which case it compacts rather than doing nothing. The released `context_compact_at` is retired in favour of `compact_at`; the old key is ignored and `collab config` says so rather than rewriting somebody's file.
* **Update**: Corrected [usage figures](collaboration/usage-figures.md) a third time, and the correction is the shape rather than the detail. The two acts each gained a MOMENT as well as a percent — `compact_when` and `new_when` — because a full window is not by itself a good time to act: a summary taken mid-turn throws away the reasoning the agent is using to finish what it is doing, and one taken at a task boundary loses nothing still needed. Both switches now ship on, with the caution moved into the percent where somebody looking for «what does collab do unasked» will find it. And a second concept sits beside it: `collab new --all` proposes a fresh session to the whole room, carried by agreement rather than authority, with every daemon reaching its own verdict from the same feed — no coordinator, and the accepted cost that two daemons may differ about who was connected when the proposal arrived.
* **Note**: The pin stays at `23db6d0`. Everything above is later than it, is said to be later than it in the prose, and carries no `verified` stamp; the bundle-wide re-pin is a separate act under the rule in [how to read this bundle](how-to-read-this-bundle.md). No new concept was created for the learnings feature for the same reason: a concept needs a `resource` pinned to the bundle's one revision, and there is nothing at that revision to cite. The viewer's roster foot changed in the same round and is recorded nowhere here, because the bundle has never described the viewer's layout: there was no true statement to correct, and inventing a concept for it would pin a claim to a revision that does not hold it.
